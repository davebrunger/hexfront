    How to play
    • Select one of your units. Bright white hexes show where it can move and still attack, faint dashed ones where it can move but not attack; red-ringed enemies can be attacked.
    • Turn sequence: each turn has three phases. Orders: click up to 4 of your units to order them (gold outline); only they act this turn. Movement: move the ordered units one at a time; once you move another unit, the previous one can't move again. Attack: attack with the ordered units, one at a time. The main button (or Space) moves on to the next phase.
    • Each ordered unit can move, then attack once per turn. Infantry can move 1 space and attack, or 2 without attacking; Armour can move up to 3 and attack; Artillery can attack, or move 1.
    • Attacking rolls one die per point of attack, which falls off with range. Each grenade, or symbol matching the target's type, does 1 damage. Targets never fire back.
    • Terrain: every hex costs 1 space to enter; lakes and rivers are impassable, and bridges can only be entered from land. Entering forest or a village ends your move, and you can't attack on the turn you moved in. Attacks on a unit in forest or a village lose 1 die for infantry and 2 for armour; attacks on a unit on a hill lose 1 die unless the attacker is on a hill too. Armour attacking from a village loses 2 dice. Artillery never loses dice to terrain, and penalties add together. An attack left with no dice can't be made.
    • Close combat: a unit next to an enemy is in close combat, and may only attack the enemies next to it, not more distant ones.
    • Retreats: each flag rolled pushes a surviving target one hex back towards its own baseline (the Allies' is the bottom edge, the Axis' the top). Terrain and obstacles don't stop a retreat, but impassable hexes, units and the map edge do; a unit always retreats as far as it can and loses 1 strength for each hex it can't. A unit on sandbags may ignore one flag. You choose where your units retreat when there's a choice.
    • Obstacles: sandbags protect a unit on all sides: infantry and armour attacking it lose 1 die (artillery doesn't), though this doesn't add to terrain cover, the larger applies. They are removed from any hex a unit leaves. Barbed wire stops a unit entering it. Armour clears it on entering and can still attack; infantry on wire lose 1 attack die, and can clear it instead of attacking.
    • Line of sight: every unit except artillery needs a clear line from its hex centre to the target's. Units, forest, villages, hills and the half hexes at the side edges block it, and so do two adjacent lake hexes. Between two units on hills, only units on hills block it. The attacker's and target's own hexes never block, and a line running along hex edges is only blocked if both sides are obstructed.
    • Zones of control: entering a hex next to an enemy ends your move.
    • The number above a target shows dice rolled, expected damage and chance to destroy it.
    • Win by destroying every enemy unit, or by having more strength left after turn 30.
    • Esc cancels, N selects the next ready unit, C clears wire, Space moves on to the next phase.
